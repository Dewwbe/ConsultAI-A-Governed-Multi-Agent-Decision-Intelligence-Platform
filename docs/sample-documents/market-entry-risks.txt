Synthetic sample for testing only. Market entry risks for a retailer expanding into the Gulf region.

Regulatory risk: licensing rules differ between free zones and the mainland, and a wrong choice can delay launch by several months. Product labelling and halal requirements apply to food and some cosmetics.

Currency risk: the dirham is pegged to the US dollar, so exchange risk against dollar costs is low, but imports priced in euros or sterling can move margins.

Supply chain risk: lead times from Asia to Jebel Ali are usually two to three weeks, but peak season congestion can double this. A regional warehouse reduces stockout risk and adds fixed cost.

Competition risk: established regional chains hold the best mall locations, and international brands often enter through franchise partners. A direct entry needs a clear price or range advantage.

Demand risk: tourism-driven sales vary by season, with a summer low. Plan working capital for two to three slow months each year.

Mitigation: start with a pilot of two or three stores, use a local partner for logistics and licensing, and set a review point at twelve months before committing to a wider rollout.
